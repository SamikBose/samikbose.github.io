<section class="section">
  <div class="wrap">
    <p class="people-intro">
      My research sits at the interface of theory, computation, and experiment, and it has been built with
      the guidance of generous mentors and the partnership of outstanding collaborators.
    </p>

    <div class="people">

      <div class="person">
        <h3>Prof. Alex Dickson</h3>
        <p class="role">Postdoctoral advisor</p>
        <p class="affil">Department of Biochemistry &amp; Molecular Biology and Department of Computational Mathematics, Science &amp; Engineering, Michigan State University</p>
        <p>
          Alex is my postdoctoral mentor and leads a group developing enhanced-sampling methods for
          long-timescale biomolecular processes — including the weighted-ensemble engine
          <em>wepy</em>, Markov state models, and conformational-space-network analysis. Under his
          mentorship I developed the merging-bias-corrected WE–MSM framework and studied inhibitor
          (un)binding kinetics in soluble epoxide hydrolase, allosteric modulation of neurolysin, and the
          Flexible Topology approach to ligand design.
        </p>
        <p class="links"><a href="https://www.egr.msu.edu/~alexrd/">Dickson Lab</a></p>
      </div>

      <div class="person">
        <h3>Prof. Debashree Ghosh</h3>
        <p class="role">Doctoral advisor</p>
        <p class="affil">School of Chemical Sciences, Indian Association for the Cultivation of Science, Kolkata</p>
        <p>
          Debashree was my PhD advisor and trained me in theoretical and quantum chemistry: polarizable
          QM/MM with the effective-fragment-potential method, the electronic structure and spectroscopy of
          biomolecules in complex environments, and machine-learning force fields. Together we explained the
          solvatochromic behaviour of green-fluorescent-protein chromophores, developed an
          interaction-energy-driven biased-sampling technique for spectral properties, and built
          support-vector-regression force fields for water.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="person">
        <h3>Dr. Benjamin J. Orlando</h3>
        <p class="role">Collaborator</p>
        <p class="affil">Department of Biochemistry &amp; Molecular Biology, Michigan State University</p>
        <p>
          Ben's group uses cryo-electron microscopy to determine the structures of membrane proteins and
          molecular machines. Our collaboration pairs his structural work with my simulations: we are
          characterizing the structure and conformational dynamics of the CbrXA transceptor and the
          mechanism of histidine transport, and we hold an NIH R21 (on which I am co-investigator) studying
          the molecular mechanisms of CbrA transceptor signaling in <em>Pseudomonas</em>.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="person">
        <h3>Prof. Augen A. Pioszak</h3>
        <p class="role">Collaborator</p>
        <p class="affil">University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center</p>
        <p>
          Augen's laboratory studies the structural biology and biochemistry of peptide-hormone receptor
          signaling. Our collaboration brings together his experimental structural and biochemical insight
          with molecular-simulation approaches to probe receptor conformational dynamics and ligand
          recognition.
        </p>
      </div>

      <div class="person">
        <h3>Prof. Kin Sing Stephen Lee</h3>
        <p class="role">Collaborator</p>
        <p class="affil">Department of Pharmacology &amp; Toxicology and Department of Chemistry, Michigan State University</p>
        <p>
          Stephen's group works on the chemical biology and pharmacology of soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH)
          and lipid-mediator signaling, with a particular interest in how drug binding kinetics and
          residence time shape efficacy. His experimentally measured residence times anchored our
          simulations of inhibitor unbinding and the analysis of ligand-binding transition states in sEH.
        </p>
      </div>

    </div>
